Quick reference while you break down film. Use it to name why a moment worked or didn’t.
🎯 Attacking Mid (9v9)
Before receiving
  • Scan over both shoulders before the ball arrives — count 2 looks minimum.
  • Receive half-turned (body open to the field), not with back to goal unless pressed.
  • Find pockets between the lines — between their mids and defenders.
  • Check to the ball to break pressure, or spin in behind when the CB steps.
On the ball
  • First touch away from pressure, into the space you scanned.
  • Look forward first: shot ▸ through ball ▸ line-breaking pass ▸ switch ▸ keep it.
  • One or two touches in tight areas; take more only when driving into open grass.
  • Good risk in the final third; safe choices in our half.
Off the ball / defending
  • Arrive late into the box on crosses and cutbacks (second wave).
  • Make third-man runs when the striker checks in.
  • Defend by screening their playmaker — stay between the ball and him.
  • Press the CB when the trigger comes (bad touch, back pass, sideline trap).
⚡ Winger (9v9)
Off the ball
  • Stay wide to stretch the defense — toes near the touchline when we build.
  • Time runs in behind the outside back; start wide, bend inside.
  • When ball is on the far side, pinch in toward the back post.
  • Create space for the fullback/mid by dragging your defender inside or deep.
On the ball
  • 1v1 is a green light in the attacking half — take defenders on.
  • First touch at the defender or past them, not backwards, when there’s space.
  • Attack the front shoulder; use speed change, not just moves.
  • Cut inside to shoot or slide the through ball; go outside to cross early or to the line.
Defending
  • Track the overlapping back — your runner is your responsibility.
  • Recover goal-side first, then press; sprint the first 5 yards of every recovery.
  • Show the attacker down the line, away from the middle.
🛡 Defensive principles (any position)
  • Goal-side, ball-side: between your man and our goal, close enough to touch.
  • Pressure – cover – balance: 1st defender presses, 2nd covers, 3rd protects the middle.
  • Press with a curved run to take away one side; don’t fly in.
  • When the ball travels, you travel — adjust every pass, don’t ball-watch.
  • Losing the ball = 5-second sprint to win it back or recover goal-side.
  • Head on a swivel in the box: ball, man, space — see two things at once.
🗣 Questions to ask him (instead of telling)
  • “Pause — what do you see? Where’s the space?”
  • “What did you look at before the ball came?”
  • “What were your three options here? Which was the bravest one that still made sense?”
  • “Where should your first touch have taken you?”
  • “If you don’t get the ball here, where do you go so the field gets bigger?”
  • “Who was your man? Could you touch him from there?”
  • “What would the best player you know do right here?”
  • End every session: 2 things he did well, 1 thing to work on — his words, not yours.
📐 Format notes (5v5 → 11v11)
  • 5v5: everyone attacks & defends; touches and 1v1s matter most. Tag technique heavily.
  • 7v7: first real shape (2-3-1 common). Introduce width and support angles.
  • 9v9 (now): lines appear — playing between them is the skill. Scanning, switching play, pressing as a unit.
  • 11v11: same principles, bigger picture — team schemes, offside timing, longer switches, set-piece roles.

How to use Film Room

Daily workflow

  1. Get the footage in. From Apple Photos: click Open video… and find your library under Media → Photos in the picker's sidebar (dragging straight out of the Photos app doesn't work — browsers never get the file; dragging from Finder or the Desktop is fine). YouTube film someone shared: press ⌘⇧5, screen-record the section, open that file here. Everything stays on your machine — nothing is uploaded.
  2. Find a moment. Use slow-mo and frame-step to land on the exact touch or decision.
  3. Draw it. Spotlight him (it follows between keyframes), arrow where the pass/run should go, zone the open space.
  4. Add a decision point just before the moment — on playback it pauses and asks him the question first.
  5. Save it as a clip with tags (👍 strength / 🔧 work-on) so it goes in his library.
  6. Export the annotated clip and AirDrop it to his iPad/phone, or review together in the app.
  7. End of the week: tick the best clips into the 🎞 highlight reel (Clips tab), order them strengths-first, and export one video for the TV — or hit 🎓 Run session to play the same list as a guided Q&A that logs his answers. Anyone can run it; the questions are built in.

Spotlights that follow a player

Auto-track: drop a spotlight on him, scrub to where the play ends and press End = playhead, scrub back, then hit 🎯 Auto-track → End. It follows him automatically and writes editable keyframes (Esc cancels). If it says it lost him — players crossing, camera whip — drag the ring back onto him at that moment and Track again from there.

By hand: scrub forward a second or two and, with the spotlight selected, drag it to where he is now (or press ＋ Keyframe here). The ring glides between keyframes, so 3–4 keyframes usually covers a whole play. Either way, give it his name and a color — and spotlight a defender or teammate in another color to show the relationship.

Saving your work

Everything autosaves in this browser per video file (matched by file name + size), so reopening the same file restores your clips and drawings. 💾 Save project downloads a backup file you can move between machines or keep with the video; 📂 Load project brings it back.

Exports — made to watch without you

🎬 Export clip renders a saved clip with every drawing burned in, at up to 1080p (looks right on a big TV). The export opens with a title card showing the rating, position, and your coaching note, and any decision point in the clip becomes a built-in freeze-frame showing the question before the play continues — so the clip teaches on its own when Mom runs it or he watches solo. The fast ⚡ mp4 mode carries the game audio over from iPhone/.mov footage, muted under the card and freezes. AirDrop the file to a phone/iPad and AirPlay it to the TV. 📸 Still saves the current frame as an image — fastest way to text him one teaching picture.

Side-by-side compare

⚖ on any clip plays it in sync next to a “model” — another clip, or an outside file (📂 in the compare bar). Both sides slow-mo and frame-step together, the pair loops, and the align nudge lines the moments up. 📸 saves a labeled two-pane image.

Tactics board

🗒 Board swaps to a top-down pitch with both teams in formation — drag the chips, double-click to name them, and draw with the same tools to teach shape and runs off-video. Each clip's 🗒 button opens a board linked to that moment. 📸 exports the board as an image.

Your words, not mine

The tag list is a starting point. In the Save-clip dialog hit ✎ Edit tag list to rename any tag to the words his coach uses, remove ones you don't use, or add your own — renames update clips you've already saved.

Zoomed-out footage

On far sideline or Trace-camera film, players are small: use Ring − on a selected spotlight to shrink the ring so it fits the player — Auto-track then automatically works at higher resolution to keep the lock. The tracker shrugs off exposure/brightness drift and coasts through another player briefly crossing in front (watch the lock number in the tracking pill — it dips and recovers). Set fps to match the footage (Trace/YouTube is usually 30).
